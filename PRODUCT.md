# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Kuala Lumpur residents reporting suspected dengue breeding habitats, plus local health officers triaging those submissions. Residents use the app on phones in the field, often outdoors, with limited time and variable lighting. Officers use a denser desktop-oriented console to verify evidence, inspect hotspot context, and record follow-up actions.

## Product Purpose
DengueWatch KL lets residents submit photo evidence of likely mosquito breeding habitats, confirm the exact map location, and track progress anonymously. The same system gives officers a queue for reviewing AI-assisted evidence, outbreak hotspot overlap, and action status. Success means faster high-quality reporting, trustworthy public awareness mapping, and an operational review surface that supports rapid response.

## Brand Personality
Clear, civic, dependable. The interface should feel like a field-ready public health tool: calm, map-led, high-contrast, and operationally credible on both phone and laptop.

## Anti-references
Avoid generic SaaS dashboards, warm editorial serif branding, decorative gradients that compete with task content, and playful consumer-social patterns that would weaken trust in a public-health workflow.

## Design Principles
1. Put the task first: primary actions and current state should be visible immediately.
2. Make public-health trust legible: privacy boundaries, consent, and status must read clearly.
3. Keep the resident flow calm and direct: each step should reduce uncertainty, not add chrome.
4. Give officers dense but stable context: queue, map, evidence, and status controls should scan quickly.
5. Use one civic visual language across mobile resident and desktop operations surfaces.
6. Prefer a map-and-data atlas feel over marketing-style panels or layered decorative shells.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast. Maintain large touch targets on resident mobile flows, strong text readability in outdoor-light conditions, keyboard accessibility for desktop review tools, and reduced reliance on color alone for statuses and warnings.
